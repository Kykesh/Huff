# YouTube Policy Compliance — Monetizing AI-Assisted Video Without Getting Killed

*Verified against current policy June 2026. Re-verify quarterly — enforcement is tightening, not loosening.*

AI content is NOT banned on YouTube. Low-effort, mass-produced content is. The channels dying in the 2026 crackdown conflated the two. This file is the line between them.

---

## The two rule systems (people conflate them — don't)

### 1. The Inauthentic Content Policy (channel-level, monetization)
July 15, 2025: YouTube retired the "repetitious content" rule and replaced it with the **inauthentic content** policy inside the YouTube Partner Program rules. January 2026: the first big enforcement wave hit. Violation costs you monetization for the whole channel, not one video.

What it targets: mass-produced, low-effort, recycled content — whether AI made it or not.

What it explicitly allows: AI as a tool that *enhances* human creativity. Videos using AI for scripts or visuals are safe when they carry **significant original input** — original commentary, unique narrative, in-depth editing, original visuals.

### 2. The Altered/Synthetic Content Disclosure (per-video, label)
Mandatory since 2024, enforced harder in 2026. In YouTube Studio's Attributes section ("AI use"), you must check Yes when content is **realistic** — something a viewer could mistake for a real person, place, scene, or event — and was made or meaningfully altered with AI.

**Undisclosed realistic AI material is itself a reused-content trigger.** The label is cheap; the strike isn't.

What does NOT need disclosure (YouTube's own examples):
- Clearly unrealistic content, animation, special effects
- Generative AI used for production assistance (scripts, ideation)
- **Charts, motion graphics, and data visualization** — clearly unrealistic by definition

What DOES need disclosure:
- Photorealistic AI imagery of real places, people, or events (an "8k cinematic skyline" of a real city qualifies — disclose it or don't use it)
- Realistic synthetic voices presented as a real person
- Realistic depictions of fictional events

Note: on sensitive topics — health, news, elections, **finance** — the label shows prominently on the video, not just in the description. Rankings channels touching GDP/wealth/economy topics should expect this. It does not hurt monetization; lying about it does.

## The 2026 kill list (what's actually getting demonetized)

Confirmed patterns from the January 2026 enforcement wave:

1. AI voiceover + stock footage, no face, no narrative voice, nothing original
2. Compilation channels reusing clips without meaningful commentary or transformation
3. News-reader channels reading articles verbatim instead of analyzing them
4. Faceless Shorts farms pushing 10+ videos/day on the same template
5. Undisclosed realistic AI material
6. Channels with sudden topic pivots that look like algorithm plays
7. Misleading metadata — titles/thumbnails the video doesn't deliver (separate, older strike vector; still live)

## The defense stack for data/rankings channels (7 layers)

Each layer is independent original value. Stack all 7 and a human reviewer has nothing to flag:

1. **Original data synthesis.** Pull from primary sources (IMF, World Bank, SIPRI, company filings), reconcile disagreements between them, and SAY which methodology you used. Wikipedia-only sourcing is the "low-effort" tell.
2. **A commentary layer on every item.** Not just "Japan: $4.2 trillion" — WHY it ranks there, what changed, what surprises. The "why" sentence per item is the single cheapest monetization insurance there is.
3. **Custom-coded visuals (Remotion).** Original motion graphics are impossible to flag as recycled and exempt from the disclosure label. This is the moat AND the shield.
4. **Sources on screen and in the description.** Citation visible in the corner during each item; full source list with URLs in the description. Trust signal for viewers, originality signal for reviewers.
5. **An original angle per video.** Every topic has 50 existing videos. Yours needs the take the others don't have — a counterintuitive finding, a fresh metric, a "the data flips here" moment. If you can't name the angle in one sentence, the video isn't ready.
6. **A human QC gate before upload.** Eyeball every video. The channels that died were the ones nobody watched before publishing.
7. **Sane cadence with a consistent identity.** 3/week in one niche looks like a creator. 10/day across pivoting topics looks like a farm.

## The 8-minute rule (the money mechanics)

- **Mid-roll ads unlock at 8:00.** Under 8 minutes you get pre-roll only; over it, you can place mid-rolls — typically 2–3x the ad revenue per view.
- Target **9:00–10:30** runtime, never under 8:30 planned (renders compress; leave buffer above the 8:00 floor).
- Place mid-rolls at natural breaks — the pattern interrupt near the midpoint of a countdown is the ideal slot. Auto-placement mid-sentence tanks retention.
- Longer runtime also compounds session watch time, which is the strongest ranking input this niche has.
- Don't pad to get there. 10 items × ~45–55 seconds of genuinely interesting material + cold open + recap lands at 9–10 minutes naturally. Padding kills retention, and retention decides whether the 8 minutes were worth anything.

## The pre-upload checklist (instantiate into your publisher/QC agent)

- [ ] Original angle present and stated (could you defend "what's original here?" in one sentence?)
- [ ] Every ranked item has at least one "why" commentary beat, not bare numbers
- [ ] Sources cited on screen AND listed with URLs in the description
- [ ] All visuals original (Remotion/custom) — zero scraped clips, zero stock-footage filler
- [ ] AI-disclosure decision made and recorded: realistic AI imagery → label YES; pure charts/motion graphics → no label needed
- [ ] Runtime ≥ 8:01 verified on the rendered file (not the script estimate)
- [ ] Title ≤ 60 chars, delivers what it promises; thumbnail matches actual content
- [ ] Data is current and timestamped on screen ("as of [month year]")
- [ ] Mid-roll slot(s) at natural breaks, not auto-placed
- [ ] A human watched the full render before upload
